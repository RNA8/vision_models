# Mixture-of-Experts for edge vision (active budget 2-6M params, <1 GMAC)

Date: 2026-10-11. Every number below was read from a page fetched in this session (arXiv abstract/ar5iv/html or a search snippet, marked "snippet"). Anything not found is marked UNVERIFIED. PDFs of V-MoE-era papers were not machine-readable, so some details (e.g. Mobile V-MoE total params, upcycling ViT tables) were not obtained.

Our baselines: dense RepViT-M0.9 (4.8M) 88.3 CIFAR-100 @224 (IN-pretrained); ViT-B teacher 93.4; own 5.4M ViT 81.5 after IN-64 pretraining; from-scratch 5.4M ViT 75.4 (C100) / 94.6 (C10).

## 2. Sparse upcycling summary

- Gains: ViT-B/L beat dense with ~50% of the original pretraining cost added; for >=1% IN 10-shot gain it needed +13% extra time vs +58% for dense continuation (https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2212.05055).
- Sweet spot +10 to +60% extra compute; very short upcycling sees an initial drop then recovers.
- No published upcycling of a Tiny/Small (<=6M) ViT or ConvNet was found. MoEE (above) is the closest conversion study and is a loss of accuracy for MAC reduction.
- Caveat for us: upcycling copies the MLP into E experts with a fresh router; at top-1 routing active MACs are unchanged, but experts initially identical, so gains come only after diversification.

## 3. Training pitfalls and fixes (sourced)

- Load balancing: V-MoE uses lambda=0.01 aux losses; "sweet spot" paper confirms 0.01 gives balanced routing but not semantic specialization (https://arxiv.org/html/2411.18322v2). Over-strong balancing forces near-uniform routing (snippet from apxml.com, a tutorial, weak source).
- Capacity/token dropping: V-MoE C=1.05 upstream; BPR keeps the highest-priority tokens when C<1; upcycling uses Expert Choice with C=2. Soft MoE removes dropping entirely.
- Router collapse: Royer+ asynchronous training pipeline and per-sample clustering init; DynamicConv temperature annealing (tau 30 -> 1, 10 epochs) because "more difficult to train"; ViMoE shared expert (78.4 -> 91.6 on C100) and restricting MoE to the last 1-2 layers.
- Small-data overfitting: CondConv "expert dropout" plus AutoAugment/Mixup; "sweet spot" paper: "more experts with smaller widths helped reduce overfitting" on IN-1k and "increased data reduces overfitting". No source tested a 50k-image from-scratch MoE ViT; treat as open.

## 4. Edge deployment reality

- Memory: total params must be resident unless experts are paged. Soft MoE: "the memory requirements of the model can grow large". M3ViT on FPGA: memory requirement reduced "2.4 times" via task-level expert loading.
- Per-image routing is the only regime where loading active experts only is sensible (Mobile V-MoE argues per-patch routing "can increase the computational and memory overhead"). Per-token routing touches nearly all experts in a 196-token image.
- Offloading is expensive for LLM MoEs: "4-20x increased inference latency" (CoSMoEs arXiv 2503.00245, snippet; LLM, flash/DRAM). Not measured for vision.
- CPU latency evidence: only DynamicConv: "about 10% more running time and 4% more Multi-Adds" (single Xeon thread). No mobile or NPU latency for vision MoE found; Mobile V-MoE lists it as future work. CondConv reports none.
- Quantization: no study on quantizing MoE ViTs found. Router top-k flips under quantization noise (snippet, arXiv 2606.05688, LLMs). Our INT8 audit (NEXT_STEPS A4) should include the router in float and check expert-choice agreement.
- Cheap-to-deploy variants ranking: per-image routed MLP experts (load 1 of E) > CondConv/DynamicConv (all K kernels aggregated per image, but one merged kernel executed; storage K-times) > Soft MoE (all experts always).

## 5. CIFAR / fine-grained MoE at <=6M active?

- Found: ViMoE C100 91.3 -> 91.6 at ~22-24M active (DINOv2 ViT-S, fine-tune). MoEE C100 DeiT-B compression. A 2025 theory paper (arXiv 2510.27004, snippet) trains a small MoE ViT from scratch on CIFAR-100 but the extracted text gave no accuracy.
- Not found: any published MoE result at <=6M active on CIFAR-10/100 or fine-grained transfer that beats a strong dense baseline. The closest <=6M evidence is ImageNet-1k from-scratch (Mobile V-MoE, DynamicConv, CondConv), all against weak-recipe dense baselines.

## 6. Honest assessment

- At fixed active budget, the evidence says MoE/conditional params do help small models more than large ones (Mobile V-MoE +3.4 to +4.7 at ViT-Tiny and below; DynamicConv +2.9 to +5.6 on MobileNets; sweet-spot paper "gains diminish for large-capacity networks"). Gains shrink when the dense baseline is well-trained (+0.3 to +0.6 on 25M+ models; +0.3 on C100 fine-tune).
- Our regime differs in two ways that cut against MoE: (a) 50k-image CIFAR from scratch is data-starved, extra total params tend to overfit and routers specialize poorly (open, unsupported by direct evidence); (b) our best model is a pretrained fine-tune where gain from extra expert capacity was +0.3 in the one analogous experiment.
- Where it could win: the 5.4M-ViT from-scratch / IN-64 pretrain track (weakest recipe, 75.4 / 81.5), where Mobile V-MoE-type gains (+2 to +4 pts) are plausible. Expected on pretrained RepViT-M0.9 fine-tune: +0 to +0.5 (my estimate, not sourced), and a total-param and latency cost.
- Deployment: only per-image routing keeps weight traffic near dense; total memory grows E-fold in the MoE layers. Probably a poor deal if the edge limit is memory, a fair deal if only MACs.
- Likelihood estimates (judgement): per-image MoE beats dense ViT 5.4M from scratch on C100 by >=1 pt: ~50%; after IN-64 pretraining: ~35%; RepViT/TinyViT fine-tune >=0.5 pt: ~15%.

## 7. Ranked experiments (1x H100)

Common metrics for all: active params, total params, active MACs (fvcore/thop with routing at inference), batch-1 CPU latency (single thread, FP32 and INT8) and peak RSS, plus expert-load entropy and per-class expert usage, 3 seeds, report the dense baseline with the same recipe.

1. **CondConv/DynamicConv-style K=4-8 experts in RepViT-M0.9's channel-mixer/pointwise convs, fine-tuned from IN weights (upcycle by replicating weights, router init near-uniform, tau=30 annealed).** Clean precedent (+1.1 to +3.0 at <10% MADDs). Cost: 30-ep 224px fine-tune ~15-35 min per variant (data-load + K-way kernel mix overhead), 4 variants (K=4/8, last-N vs all layers) ~2 h. Also gives the first CPU latency datapoint to compare with DynamicConv's +10%.
2. **Per-image-routed MLP-MoE (Mobile V-MoE style) in our 5.4M ViT, from scratch on CIFAR-100/10.** E=4/8, k=1 per image, last 2-4 blocks only, shared expert, balance loss 0.01, expert dropout, optional super-class (CIFAR-100 has 20 coarse labels, a free super-class signal). Active params ~5.4M, total ~8-12M. 300 ep ~1.2-1.8 h per run; 6 configs (E, layers, shared yes/no) ~8-10 h, runnable 3-4 in parallel.
3. **Same, after IN-64 pretraining (MoE during pretraining vs upcycle at fine-tune start).** Test whether MoE gain survives the 81.5 starting point. Pretrain with MoE ~6-8 h (IN-64, extra ~20% for routing), or upcycle the existing IN-64 checkpoint (E=8, +10-30% of pretraining = ~1-2 h) then 100-ep C100 finetune ~30 min. Compare to continued dense training with the same extra compute (the upcycling paper's control).
4. **Soft MoE in the MLPs of the 5.4M ViT from scratch (slots = tokens/experts, E=8-16).** Gradient-friendly, no balance loss; likely best accuracy but loads all experts per image, so report memory. 300-ep ~1.5-2 h per run, 2-3 configs ~5 h. Serves as an accuracy upper bound for the routed variant in item 2.
5. **Sparse upcycling of TinyViT-5M (21k-distilled weights) MLPs with E=4-8, last-2 blocks plus shared expert, fine-tune 30 ep at 224.** Expected small (+0 to +0.5, ViMoE precedent +0.3); run only if item 1 shows gain. ~30-60 min per run, 4 runs ~3 h. Include a "random-router, no-balance" ablation to guard the 78.4 vs 91.6 failure mode.

Do first: items 2 and 1 in parallel (different slots), then 3. Stop rule: if item 2 at the best config is not >=+1 pt over dense with 3 seeds, drop MoE for the from-scratch track.
