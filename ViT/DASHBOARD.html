The story, step by step

Thirteen chapters in the order the work happened. Each one states the question, the exact command-line change, the mechanism, the result, the lesson, and how it compares with published work.

01

A fast pipeline for tiny models

first run Oct 10 15:00

Question

A 5M-parameter ViT on 32×32 images finishes a forward and backward pass in milliseconds. Is the GPU doing the work, or is it waiting on the data loader and on Python?

What we changed

  • The whole dataset lives on the GPU as a uint8 tensor, so no CPU data loader runs. Crop, flip, RandAugment, MixUp/CutMix and random erasing all run batched on the GPU.
  • bf16 autocast and torch.compile (kernel fusion).
  • --cudagraphs: compile with mode=reduce-overhead, which records the training step once and replays it.
  • Up to four training queues share the one H100 at the same time.

Why it should matter

Small models are launch-bound. Each CUDA kernel finishes faster than Python can issue the next one, so the GPU idles between kernels. A CUDA graph records the whole sequence of kernel launches once and replays it with a single launch, which removes that per-kernel CPU overhead. The win shrinks as batches grow, because big kernels hide the launch cost. For the same reason one small job leaves most of an 80 GB, 350 W GPU unused, so several jobs can run side by side.

Result

On a real 10-epoch CIFAR-10 run (batch 256), CUDA graphs cut 9.45 to 3.48 s/epoch, a 2.7× speedup, at the same accuracy (53.2% vs 53.0% val). At batch 1024 the gain is 1.6×. With three concurrent jobs, each job's epoch got 9 to 55% slower, but total throughput roughly doubled. The GPU was using 9 of 80 GB and 226 of 350 W.

Measured cost of skipping graphs: t5c_bs256 ran ungraphed at 5,198 img/s and took 16.5 min.

Lesson

Profile before tuning the model. For small vision transformers, the fixed cost of launching kernels can dominate. Removing it bought 2.7× on every later experiment, and parallel queues bought another ~2×.

Against published results

Our pipeline is built for many short ablations, not for records. airbench94: 94% in 3.29 s on one A100 reaches 94% on CIFAR-10 in 3.29 s with a small CNN. Our best ViT needs about 40 to 80 minutes for 93 to 94.5%.

02

The regularization ladder on CIFAR-10

first run Oct 10 15:00

Question

A plain ViT-Tiny (12 layers, width 192, 3 heads, patch 4) has no built-in notion of locality. With only 50k images, how badly does it overfit, and which regularizer fixes it?

What we changed

One change per rung, 100 epochs, batch 1024, lr 2e-3 (the defaults):

train_vit.py --run-name t1_plain --wandb --eval-every 5 --model tiny --epochs 100 --label-smoothing 0 --weight-decay 0 --mixup 0 --cutmix 0 --drop-path 0
train_vit.py --run-name t2_ls_wd --wandb --eval-every 5 --model tiny --epochs 100 --mixup 0 --cutmix 0 --drop-path 0
train_vit.py --run-name t3_randaug --wandb --eval-every 5 --model tiny --epochs 100 --mixup 0 --cutmix 0 --drop-path 0 --randaug
train_vit.py --run-name t4_mix --wandb --eval-every 5 --model tiny --epochs 100 --drop-path 0 --randaug
train_vit.py --run-name t5_droppath --wandb --eval-every 5 --model tiny --epochs 100 --randaug
train_vit.py --run-name t5b_mix_half --wandb --eval-every 5 --randaug --model tiny --epochs 100 --mix-prob 0.5
train_vit.py --run-name t5c_bs256 --wandb --eval-every 5 --randaug --model tiny --epochs 100 --batch-size 256 --lr 1e-3
train_vit.py --run-name t6_full_300ep --wandb --eval-every 5 --model tiny --epochs 300 --randaug

Why it should matter

  • Memorization: a model with 5M parameters can store 50k labels. Train accuracy hits 100% while validation stalls. The gap between the two is the overfitting signal.
  • RandAugment creates new plausible images every step, so memorizing gets harder.
  • MixUp/CutMix blends two images and their labels. It is a strong regularizer, which also makes the training task harder: the model needs many more steps to fit it.
  • Step count: at batch 1024, 100 epochs is only ~4.9k optimizer steps. Going to batch 256 gives 4× the updates for the same number of images seen.

Result

t1_plain reaches 100.00% train but 82.90% val, a 17.1-point gap. RandAugment adds +2.89 points and shrinks the gap to 7.2. Adding MixUp/CutMix drops val by -15.04 points, but train accuracy falls to 72.89%: the model now underfits. Mixing only half the batches recovers +7.11; four times the steps (batch 256) recovers +10.12; 300 epochs reaches 87.96%.

Lesson

Read the train curve and the val curve together. A falling val number can mean overfitting or underfitting, and the fix is opposite in each case. Strong augmentation needs a long schedule. At batch 1024 the model was starved of optimizer steps, not over-regularized.

Against published results

The matched published baseline (same 192/12/3 p4 shape), ViT-Tiny 192/12/3 p4, plain init, gets 88.43% at 100 epochs. Our 300-epoch 87.96% is within 0.5 points of it, and 5.6 points below tuned small ViTs such as ViT-Lite-7/4 (CCT paper) (93.57%).

03

Scale up: width, learning rate and tokens

first run Oct 10 15:38

Question

If ViT-Tiny overfits, does a wider ViT-Small (21M) do better? And what does patch size, which sets the number of tokens, buy?

What we changed

train_vit.py --run-name s_small_200ep --wandb --eval-every 5 --randaug --model small --epochs 200
train_vit.py --run-name s_small_200ep_lr1e-3 --wandb --eval-every 5 --randaug --model small --epochs 200 --lr 1e-3
train_vit.py --run-name p2_tiny --wandb --eval-every 5 --randaug --model tiny --epochs 100 --patch-size 2
train_vit.py --run-name p8_tiny --wandb --eval-every 5 --randaug --model tiny --epochs 100 --patch-size 8

Why it should matter

Learning rate must shrink as width grows. With Adam every weight moves by about the learning rate each step, so a wider layer, which sums more inputs, changes its output more per step. A rate that is safe at width 192 can be too large at width 384 and the loss plateaus. Patch size sets sequence length: a 32×32 image cut into p×p patches gives (32/p)² tokens. Patch 2 means 256 tokens and roughly 4× the attention compute of patch 4; patch 8 gives only 16 tokens and loses spatial detail.

Result

ViT-Small at lr 2e-3 stalled near loss 2.1 and 58.03% val. Halving lr to 1e-3 gave 78.64% (+20.61), so the stall was an optimization failure. Even fixed, Small (200 ep) is below Tiny at batch 256 (82.09%). Patch 2 is +3.04 over patch 4 at 0.31× the throughput; patch 8 loses -14.59.

Lesson

Scale the learning rate down when you scale width up, and treat a flat loss curve as a learning-rate suspect before calling it a capacity limit. More parameters did not help at 50k images.

Against published results

No published ViT-Small CIFAR-10 number is in our verified notes. The CCT paper's ViT-12/16 (CCT paper) (86M, patch 16) gets 83.04%, which agrees with the trend: bigger and coarser is not better on small data.

04

The ResNet-18 control

first run Oct 10 15:30

Question

Is the ViT's weak result a property of the data and budget, or of the architecture? Train a ResNet-18 with the identical heavy recipe and compare.

What we changed

train_vit.py --run-name r18_full --wandb --eval-every 5 --model resnet18 --epochs 100 --randaug
train_vit.py --run-name c100_r18_full --wandb --eval-every 5 --dataset cifar100 --batch-size 256 --lr 1e-3 --cudagraphs --model resnet18 --epochs 100 --randaug
train_vit.py --run-name tin_r18_full --wandb --eval-every 5 --dataset tiny_imagenet --batch-size 256 --lr 1e-3 --cudagraphs --model resnet18 --epochs 100 --randaug --r18-stem stride2
train_vit.py --run-name in64_r18_full --wandb --eval-every 2 --log-every-steps 500 --warmup-epochs 2 --dataset imagenet64 --patch-size 8 --batch-size 256 --lr 1e-3 --cudagraphs --model resnet18 --epochs 30 --randaug --r18-stem stride2

Why it should matter

A convolution looks only at a small neighbourhood and uses the same weights at every position. Those two assumptions (locality and translation equivariance) are true of natural images, so the network does not have to learn them from data. That is called inductive bias. A ViT starts with neither and must learn them from examples, which costs data and steps.

Result

CIFAR-10: ResNet-18 93.16% in 3.0 min vs ViT-Tiny 71.97% (+21.19). The gap is +15.91 on CIFAR-100, +14.97 on Tiny-ImageNet and +5.06 on ImageNet-64 (30 epochs, 1.28M images).

Lesson

At short budgets the CNN wins everywhere. The margin shrinks as the dataset grows: with 1.28M images the ViT has more examples to learn locality from.

Against published results

Our ResNet-18 (93.16%) matches ResNet18 (kuangliu/pytorch-cifar) (93.02%) and is 2.1 points under the tuned Cutout-paper ResNet-18 (95.28%, 200 ep, batch 128).

05

Matching published pure ViTs

first run Oct 10 17:58

Question

Published pure ViTs reach about 93.6 to 94.5% on CIFAR-10 at 3M parameters. Our ViT-Tiny was at 87.96%. Can we reproduce their shape and recipe?

What we changed

The SL-ViT shape (9 layers, width 192, 12 heads of dim 16, MLP ratio 2, 2.7M params) and recipe (batch 128, warmup, random erasing). Then LSA, SeqPool and impulse init, and finally the learning rate:

train_vit.py --run-name bf1_slvit --wandb --eval-every 5 --dataset cifar10 --cudagraphs --model slvit --batch-size 128 --lr 3e-3 --weight-decay 0.05 --warmup-epochs 10 --randaug --label-smoothing 0.1 --drop-path 0.1 --reprob 0.25 --epochs 100
train_vit.py --run-name bf1_slvit_lr5e-4 --wandb --eval-every 5 --dataset cifar10 --cudagraphs --model slvit --batch-size 128 --weight-decay 0.05 --warmup-epochs 10 --randaug --label-smoothing 0.1 --drop-path 0.1 --reprob 0.25 --epochs 100 --lr 5e-4
train_vit.py --run-name bf3b_slvit_300ep_lr5e-4 --wandb --eval-every 5 --dataset cifar10 --cudagraphs --model slvit --batch-size 128 --lr 3e-3 --weight-decay 0.05 --warmup-epochs 10 --randaug --label-smoothing 0.1 --drop-path 0.1 --reprob 0.25 --epochs 300 --warmup-epochs 30 --init impulse --mix-off-epoch 175 --pool seqpool --lsa --lr 5e-4
train_vit.py --run-name bf4_slvit_300ep_mixhalf --wandb --eval-every 5 --dataset cifar10 --cudagraphs --model slvit --batch-size 128 --lr 3e-3 --weight-decay 0.05 --warmup-epochs 10 --randaug --label-smoothing 0.1 --drop-path 0.1 --reprob 0.25 --epochs 300 --warmup-epochs 30 --init impulse --mix-off-epoch 175 --pool seqpool --lsa --lr 5e-4 --mix-prob 0.5 --mixup 1.0 --cutmix 1.0

Why it should matter

  • LSA (locality self-attention) masks each token's attention to itself and learns the softmax temperature. Attention gets sharper and has to look at neighbours.
  • SeqPool classifies from an attention-weighted average of all tokens instead of a single CLS token.
  • Impulse init sets the query/key weights so each head starts out attending to a fixed nearby offset, like a convolution filter.
  • Mix-off at epoch 175 turns MixUp/CutMix off for the last 40% so the model can fit clean images.
  • Batch 128 means 8× the optimizer steps per epoch of batch 1024, which is what chapter 2 said we lacked.

Result

The paper's lr 3e-3 gave 75.84%, and with impulse init or the Tiny shape it stalled at ~49% train accuracy. Lowering lr fixed it: 1e-3 → 82.40, 5e-4 → 83.67. At 300 epochs, lr 3e-3 reached 90.40% and lr 5e-4 (bf3b) reached 93.37%. Lighter mixing (bf4) gave 93.44%.

Lesson

A published recipe is tied to its own pipeline. Their lr 3e-3 assumed their sampler (3 augmented copies per image, ~117k steps). Without those repeats, the same lr was too high. Rerun a short learning-rate sweep whenever you port a recipe.

Against published results

bf3b 93.37% vs ViT-Lite-7/4 (CCT paper) 93.57 matched -0.20 and ViT baseline, SPT/LSA paper 93.58 matched -0.21. It is still below SL-ViT (ViT + SPT + LSA) (94.53), which also uses shifted patch tokenization (SPT). We never implemented SPT.

Question

bf3b changed five things at once versus our ViT-Tiny. Which ones mattered? Undo exactly one per run, and repeat seeds to know what a real difference is.

What we changed

train_vit.py --run-name abl_no_lsa --wandb --eval-every 5 --dataset cifar10 --cudagraphs --model slvit --batch-size 128 --lr 5e-4 --weight-decay 0.05 --epochs 300 --warmup-epochs 30 --randaug --label-smoothing 0.1 --drop-path 0.1 --reprob 0.25 --init impulse --mix-off-epoch 175 --pool seqpool

train_vit.py --run-name abl_tiny_shape --wandb --eval-every 5 --dataset cifar10 --cudagraphs --model tiny --batch-size 128 --lr 5e-4 --weight-decay 0.05 --epochs 300 --warmup-epochs 30 --randaug --label-smoothing 0.1 --drop-path 0.1 --reprob 0.25 --init impulse --mix-off-epoch 175 --pool seqpool --lsa
train_vit.py --run-name bf3b_seed1 --wandb --eval-every 5 --dataset cifar10 --cudagraphs --model slvit --batch-size 128 --lr 5e-4 --weight-decay 0.05 --epochs 300 --warmup-epochs 30 --randaug --label-smoothing 0.1 --drop-path 0.1 --reprob 0.25 --init impulse --mix-off-epoch 175 --pool seqpool --lsa --seed 1
train_vit.py --run-name bf3b_seed2 --wandb --eval-every 5 --dataset cifar10 --cudagraphs --model slvit --batch-size 128 --lr 5e-4 --weight-decay 0.05 --epochs 300 --warmup-epochs 30 --randaug --label-smoothing 0.1 --drop-path 0.1 --reprob 0.25 --init impulse --mix-off-epoch 175 --pool seqpool --lsa --seed 2

Why it should matter

A bundle of changes only tells you the sum. A one-change ablation tells you each part's share, and only if the differences beat seed noise. Three seeds of bf3b give a standard deviation of 0.11 points, so differences under about 0.3 points are noise.

Result

Removing LSA costs -2.71 points, by far the biggest drop. CLS instead of SeqPool costs -0.40, default init -0.80. Swapping the SL-ViT shape for the plain ViT-Tiny shape (12 layers, 3 heads, MLP 4) with the same recipe gains +1.17 points to 94.54%, at 5.4M vs 2.7M params.

Lesson

We credited the jump from 88 to 93% to the SL-ViT shape. The controlled test says otherwise: the recipe (lr, batch 128, long schedule, mix-off) and LSA did the work, and the shape we abandoned is better when trained properly. Bundled comparisons in the literature (a different shape and recipe) can mislead in the same way.

Against published results

ViT-Tiny shape + bf3b recipe: 94.54% vs SL-ViT (ViT + SPT + LSA) 94.53 matched +0.01, close to the structured-init ViT-Tiny (94.67%, 300 ep). Note the comparison is not size-matched: 5.4M vs 2.9M params.

first run Oct 10 16:47

Question

Do the CIFAR-10 lessons transfer when there are 100 classes (500 images each), 200 classes at 64 px, or 1.28M images?

What we changed

The same ladder at batch 256 with sqrt-scaled lr (1e-3; ViT-Small 5e-4); patch 8 at 64 px keeps 64 tokens. Then the bf3b recipe on CIFAR-100:

train_vit.py --run-name c100_t3_randaug --wandb --eval-every 5 --dataset cifar100 --batch-size 256 --lr 1e-3 --cudagraphs --model tiny --epochs 100 --mixup 0 --cutmix 0 --drop-path 0 --randaug
train_vit.py --run-name c100_tinyshape_bf3b --wandb --eval-every 5 --cudagraphs --dataset cifar100 --model tiny --batch-size 128 --lr 5e-4 --weight-decay 0.05 --epochs 300 --warmup-epochs 30 --randaug --label-smoothing 0.1 --drop-path 0.1 --reprob 0.25 --init impulse --mix-off-epoch 175 --pool seqpool --lsa
train_vit.py --run-name tin_t5_droppath --wandb --eval-every 5 --dataset tiny_imagenet --batch-size 256 --lr 1e-3 --cudagraphs --model tiny --epochs 100 --patch-size 8 --randaug
train_vit.py --run-name in64_s_small_full --wandb --eval-every 2 --log-every-steps 500 --warmup-epochs 2 --dataset imagenet64 --patch-size 8 --batch-size 256 --lr 1e-3 --cudagraphs --model small --epochs 40 --randaug --lr 5e-4

Why it should matter

Fewer images per class make memorizing easier and learning general features harder, so the train-val gap grows. More total data has the opposite effect: with ImageNet's 1.28M images the ViT can learn locality from examples, so the CNN's built-in advantage matters less.

Result

CIFAR-100 plain ViT: 99.97% train, 57.33% val, a 42.6-point gap. RandAugment again helps most (+5.05). The bf3b recipe reaches 75.69%; the Tiny-shape version is 75.16. ResNet-18 gets 73.22% in 3.6 min. On Tiny-ImageNet, ResNet-18 leads the best ViT by 8.3 points. On ImageNet-64, ViT-Small (40 ep) reaches 57.89% and passes ResNet-18 (30 ep, 51.23%).

Lesson

The ViT-vs-CNN gap depends on data size per inductive bias you leave out. On small datasets, build locality in (LSA, a conv stem) or bring pretrained weights. On ImageNet scale, the ViT catches up even at short budgets. The ImageNet-64 comparison is not epoch-matched (40 vs 30).

Against published results

CIFAR-100: 75.69% vs ViT baseline, SPT/LSA paper 73.81 beat +1.88, ViT-Lite-7/4 73.94 beat +1.75, SL-ViT 76.92 below -1.23. The hybrid CCT-7/3x1 (hybrid) gets 80.92, and the from-scratch record is PyramidNet-272 + ShakeDrop + AA + CutMix + ASAM at 89.9 with 26M params and 900 epochs. Research track: a recurrent-depth ViT

first run Oct 10 18:36

Question

Can one shared block, looped r times, match a deeper ViT at the same parameter count? Does it keep improving when looped past its training depth, and can each image stop when it is done?

What we changed

Prelude (2 blocks) embeds the image; a core (2 blocks, or 1) is looped r times, with r sampled per batch around a mean of 4 or 8 and gradients through only the last 4 loops; a coda (2 blocks) and head classify. Injection feeds the prelude output back into every loop, by addition (add), by a concat+linear adapter (concat) or not at all. Deep supervision (--rd-coda-every-step) applies the loss after every loop.

Lesson

Seeds (final-epoch val): CIFAR-100 SL-ViT shape, 1000 ep: 79.03 ± 0.65 (n=3: 79.78, 78.62, 78.69); CIFAR-100 SL-ViT + SPT, 1000 ep: 80.91 ± 0.26 (n=3: 80.66, 81.17, 80.91); CIFAR-10 ViT-Tiny shape, 1000 ep: 96.54 ± 0.13 (n=3: 96.39, 96.61, 96.61); CIFAR-10 ViT-Tiny + SPT, 1000 ep: 96.87 ± 0.04 (n=3: 96.86, 96.92, 96.84); CIFAR-10 SL-ViT + SPT (2.75M), 1000 ep: 96.75 ± 0.26 (n=3: 97.03, 96.51, 96.71); CIFAR-100 SL-ViT + SPT distilled from own 3-seed ensemble, 1000 ep: 82.86 ± 0.02 (n=2: 82.88, 82.85).

Schedule length was the biggest single lever left. The 2.7M SL-ViT-shape pure ViT reached 79.78 on CIFAR-100 (+4.1 over 300 epochs) and the 5.4M ViT-Tiny shape reached 96.39 on CIFAR-10 (+1.9; seed means in the line above), above every single-stage, CIFAR-only pure ViT we found, and level with the two-stage self-supervised ViT of Gani et al. (79.15 / 96.41). On CIFAR-100 that is below the CCT conv-tokenizer hybrid at 300 epochs; on CIFAR-10 the 3-seed mean (96.54) matches it (96.53). On CIFAR-100 the smaller 2.7M shape beat the 5.4M one by 2.3 points. The recurrent model's 2-point lead over the flat model at 100 epochs disappeared at 500 epochs (77.44 vs 77.86): looping mostly bought faster convergence, not a better optimum, and the looped model costs more MACs. Adding SL-ViT's shifted patch tokenization (SPT) gave 76.70 at 300 epochs (+1.0) and 80.66 at 1000 epochs (3-seed mean in the line above, about +1.9 over the no-SPT mean), level with the CCT hybrid at 300 epochs (80.92) while staying a pure ViT. On CIFAR-10, SPT on the ViT-Tiny shape gave 96.86 (3-seed mean in the line above), above CCT at 300 epochs (96.53). Clean train accuracy is 99.9%+ for the 1000-epoch runs.

Held-out check: the recipes were chosen while watching the test set, so the CIFAR-100 SPT recipe was rerun on 45k images with a fixed 5k held-out split (--holdout 5000), scoring the test set once at the end: held-out val 79.88 vs test 79.89. Selection did not overfit the test set; the run is about 1 point lower because it trains on 10% less data. CIFAR-10 SPT recipe, same check: held-out 96.80 vs test 96.80.

Against published results

CCT trains 1500 to 5000 epochs for its best numbers, so comparing at matched epochs matters: at 1000 epochs our best pure ViT (with SPT) is 80.66 vs CCT's 82.72 at 1500 (hybrid, from the paper's long-schedule table, see research/long_runs.md).
